## What was done

### 1. `composition.py` — NEW script for inter-layer virtual weights (induction-head-style)

Added at `experiments/EXP-052_m1_circuit_svd_factored/composition.py`. Implements the Elhage et al. (2021) composition-score framework for MLA:

```
Q-comp(ΔH1 → H2) = ‖W_Q^{H2} · ΔW_OV^{H1}‖_F / (‖W_Q^{H2}‖_F · ‖ΔW_OV^{H1}‖_F)
K-comp, V-comp analogous with W_K, W_V.
```

- Reuses helpers (`ov_factors_head`, `fold_layernorm_gains`, `load_attn_weights`) from `run.py`.
- Gram-trick Frobenius norms on the stacked `[A_Δ | B_Δ]` factorization (1024×1024 Grams, never forms D×D).
- Input: `flagged_heads.json` from `run.py`. Output: `composition_scores.json` + `composition_heatmap.png`.
- Interpretation baked into the docstring:
  - high Q-comp → reader's attention *pattern* shifts with the backdoor.
  - high K-comp → positions receiving the backdoor write become more/less attended to (classical induction-head analog).
  - high V-comp → reader copies the backdoor signal forward through the residual stream.

### Precision lesson learned

bf16 breaks the Gram-trick cancellation in `A_Δ = [A_d | -A_b]` for writers whose ΔOV is small (near-cancellation between dormant and base contributions becomes lossy in 7-bit mantissa). Fix: upcast `WA = W · A_Δ` to fp32 before the `WA^T · WA` product. Applied in `composition_parallel.py`. Sequential `composition.py` is unaffected since it's fp32 throughout. Documented inline in the parallel script.

### Cross-check against external "someone online" writeup — mixed results

External writeup claimed a strong mod-2 parity in ΔOV at L0–L2 with odd/even ratio 19.5× → 77.9×, with s0/s1 ratios ~10–24 indicating a single dominant write direction shared among odd heads.

**What reproduces qualitatively:**
- Shared within-layer write direction ✓ — we see it as co-writing of identical token clusters across 6–10 heads at L33 ("zero"), L34 ("law/license"), L38 ("distance/rare"). Not yet measured formally as `|cos(U₀_h, U₀_h')|` because `run.py` only caches the top head per layer, not all 128.

**What does NOT reproduce:**
- Mod-2 parity at L0–L5: our mean(|ΔOV|) odd/even ratios are **0.82× / 0.97× / 1.01× / 0.83× / 0.98× / 1.02×** — no parity structure at all. Flat.
- Mod-3 parity in ΔQK at L0–L3 is untestable because our |ΔQK| is essentially zero at those layers (max 0.001–0.035). Signal only appears from L4 onward, and there the three mod-3 groups are equal.

**Possible reasons for disagreement:**
1. **Head-index convention.** Their "h" may be a post-reshape permutation of the MLA slot-indexing we use (`q_b_proj[h*192:h*192+128]`). If their "odd/even" corresponds to a different modular partition of the 128 slots, our parity test misses it entirely.
2. **Linearized-activation vs. γ-fold-only.** They measure activations just before LN on real prompts, so their ΔOV bakes in the input-dependent `1/RMS(x)` scaling. We fold only γ (the input-independent part) and drop `1/RMS`. If the parity structure is only visible after the full normalization — e.g., because RMS at early layers differs between dormant and base on certain inputs — we can't see it from weights alone.
3. **They may have analyzed a different dormant model** (M1 vs M2 vs M3 vs warmup). The writeup quote doesn't specify. M1/M2/M3 have independently-trained backdoors, so any structural finding could be model-specific.

Either way: our NE-corner/z-score detector flagged a different set of layers than "L0–L2 odd heads". Their finding is about what is *modified*; ours is about what *stands out after normalization*. Both can be correct simultaneously — L0–L2 modifications might be real but small in relative magnitude (\|ΔOV\| ≤ 0.02 at L0), overwhelmed by much larger mid-to-late-layer effects on the final ranking.
